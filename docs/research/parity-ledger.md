# Free and premium feature ledger

Research dates: 9 to 10 October 2026. This comparison covers Enhancv's free tools, free access, Pro features and separately documented Business features. ClearCV's implemented features don't require a subscription.

The sources are official product and Help Centre pages read without signing in. They show what Enhancv advertises, but don't prove how its editor works or how effective it is. This research used no private accounts, paid access, proprietary code or Enhancv templates. The [REA investigation record](rea-provenance.md) describes the separate browser observations.

The table keeps the status recorded during earlier development. Later selected-text formatting, two-column design, page previews, reviewed whole-job drafts, reusable letters and the application board are described in [consumer workflows](../consumer-workflows.md). Those additions don't establish proprietary template, algorithm or service parity. Read the historical labels below as follows:

- **Baseline implemented:** part of the initial ClearCV version, with checks recorded in [verification](../verification.md).
- **Coordinator confirms implementation:** added during the expansion, with final browser, print, Linux and macOS checks still required before release.
- **Module verified:** the underlying data operations passed `tests/review.test.js`; this alone doesn't check the interface.
- **Remaining:** a feature ClearCV doesn't yet provide.
- **Unknown:** something the public research couldn't establish.

The expanded interface is integrated, but separate browser and platform checks are still required. These labels don't establish a complete match between products.

## What the official pages say about plans

The [free-plan Help Centre article](https://help.enhancv.com/en/articles/1208865-what-is-included-in-the-free-version), updated 5 February 2026, describes seven days of free access, all templates and design tools, basic sections, branding and a maximum of 12 section items. It lists Pro as supporting 300 resumes and cover letters, real-time content suggestions, an ATS check, Pro sections, removal of branding, unlimited section items and additional design options. It also says people can try most Pro features during free access. The page doesn't describe an unlimited, permanently free editor.

The [pricing page](https://enhancv.com/pricing/) lists those features, but its extracted text returned `NaN` and zero values for the paid price. This observation leaves exact paid amounts **unknown**. Prices haven't been guessed from the broken values, a blog or another region.

Individual tools have separate availability statements. The [cover-letter generator](https://enhancv.com/cover-letter-generator/) says it is free without a login. The [job tracker](https://enhancv.com/features/job-application-tracker/) says it is free with an account. Those statements apply to those tools, rather than every feature in the subscribed editor.

## How document upgrades work

Document upgrades use the existing CV and workspace format. Reading a file doesn't edit a saved CV. You compare the extracted text with the suggested fields, then create a new CV or choose how to merge the details into a separate copy. Existing CVs, snapshots, master profile, applications and submitted copies stay in place. Entries are skipped as duplicates only when their content matches exactly; differences in case and spacing are kept. Ambiguous or oversized text remains in the source with a warning for you to check.

The app bundles document readers, fonts, dictionaries and OCR language data. Importing doesn't make automatic provider calls. PDFs and images can be up to 10 MB, with a maximum of 20 PDF pages or 32 million source image pixels. Other documents can be up to 2 MB, and extracted text up to 50,000 characters. DOCX imports read referenced headers, footers, notes and text boxes, keeping useful link addresses as text. Notices identify unsupported parts of older Word files. Passwords stay in memory, and cancelling discards unfinished results.

The [document-upgrade guide](../document-upgrades.md) covers supported formats, retained source text and spelling limits. Model tests check the data operations. Separate browser, OCR, layout and opened-export checks are needed to show how they work together.

## How application records work

The application tracker was written independently using official public descriptions checked on 10 October 2026 and the previous source comparison. Application records gain optional `archived`, `tasks`, `customFields` and `submissions` fields in the existing versioned workspace. Missing fields get defaults; malformed fields cause the whole import to be rejected. Earlier CV and workspace fields are kept. Recovery for unreadable workspaces and the original legacy browser value keep their existing protection. The active CV format and optional shared-document format don't change. Applications stay local.

Submitted copies keep the complete CV and letter separately from the editable originals. They can't be edited or replaced directly. You can delete one, or create a new editable library document from it. `submittedAt` records the capture time in UTC and is displayed in local time. Applied and due dates use the supplied `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date.

Statistics use the browser's local date, exclude archived records and cover today plus the previous six days. They don't infer a history of stage changes. Completed tasks stop counting as pending; the older single follow-up date stays pending until cleared. Limits remain 150 application records, with 30 tasks, 20 custom fields and 8 submitted copies per record, within the shared 4 MB workspace.

`src/tracker.js` provides validation, `filterApplications`, `applicationAnalytics` and `applicationsToCsv`. CSV quotes every cell and adds an apostrophe before formula or control prefixes. It includes record text, custom fields, task summaries and submitted-copy details, but leaves out the saved CV and letter contents. Use workspace JSON for a full backup; spreadsheet import isn't available. Custom labels are case-sensitive, and repeated labels in one record join their values with line breaks. Model and helper tests pass under the recorded Node runtime. Separate browser, print and platform checks are still required before release.

## How local writing checks work

`src/review.js` provides `REVIEW_RULES`, `reviewCV(doc)`, `tailoringSuggestions(doc, jobAd = doc.jobAd)` and `coverLetterDraft(doc, company = '', role = '')`. These functions leave their input unchanged and make no external calls.

- `reviewCV` returns `method`, `issues`, `metrics` and a separate copy of `rules`. Each issue has `ruleId`, `severity` (`attention` or `suggestion`), `path`, `message`, `suggestion` and an `evidence` excerpt from the supplied text. A path identifies a document property; `description:2` means its second original line. Hidden sections are excluded from checks and word counts. Profile fields are still checked. There is no combined score.
- Writing thresholds: fewer than 15 or more than 90 summary words; more than 40 words in one description line; more than eight lines for one experience; fewer than 100 or more than 1,000 visible CV words. These are editing prompts, not page limits or suitability judgements.
- The small phrase lists are available in the source: summary phrases `hard-working`, `results-oriented`/`results-orientated`, `team player`, `go-getter`, `self-motivated`, `dynamic professional`; responsibility openings `responsible for`, `duties included`, `worked on`, `helped`/`helped with`, `assisted with`. Case, spaces and supported hyphens are normalised as documented in the source.
- The number check looks for a digit in an experience description. It can't establish achievement or accuracy, and qualitative detail is still useful. A missing digit prompts you to add verifiable detail if relevant; it doesn't invent a metric.
- Dates accept years, English month/year or `YYYY-MM`, a single completion date or a range ending in a date, `Present`, `Current` or `Now`. Other formats are flagged for you to check. Consistency checks don't invent months, require chronological ordering, use the system clock or assess career gaps.
- `tailoringSuggestions` returns `method`, `keywords`, `found`, `missing`, `experienceOrder`, `skillOrder`, `suggestions` and `rankings`. The order arrays contain existing IDs only. Each ranking gives `id` and `matchedKeywords`; each suggestion gives `path`, `message` and `matchedKeywords`. It compares all stored text, including custom and hidden sections, with up to 24 frequent job-description words after applying ClearCV's common-word filter. Matches must be exact. The number of different matching words determines the order; tied entries keep their original order. It doesn't match word stems, compare meaning or give a hiring score.
- `coverLetterDraft` returns `method`, editable full `text`, structured `body`, `salutation`, `closing`, `warnings` and `usedPaths`. It uses the supplied summary, first non-empty experience (or education), up to six skill names and the signature. The opening uses the supplied role or headline and company where available. Missing facts are left out and listed in warnings. It doesn't add placeholders, guessed motivations, invented outcomes or claims taken from the job advertisement.

`node --test tests/review.test.js` passed 17 checks during module handoff on Linux. They covered date formats, repeated and long lines, hidden and custom content, contact details, stable exact-word ranking, unchanged inputs, separate rule copies and letter drafts built from supplied facts. These checks don't establish interface, PDF or macOS behaviour for the expanded release.

## What still needs checking

The comparison covers more features than ClearCV provides. Signed-in workflows, regional plan restrictions, the complete template and section catalogue, import compatibility, AI correctness and remote services remain unknown or incomplete. Module tests can't prove those behaviours. Keep the gaps in the ledger and record separate integration checks before calling a feature complete.
